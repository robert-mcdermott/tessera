The API

Everything the web app shows comes from a JSON API under /api/v1, and the same API is open to scripts and other tools. A running Tessera documents it at /api/docs; the OpenAPI document is at /api/openapi.json, and a copy for this version is in openapi.json.

Without accounts (single-operator mode), the API answers only on this machine and needs no sign-in. With accounts on, a script signs in like the web app:

curl -s -c jar -H "Content-Type: application/json" -X POST http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/session \
  -d '{"username": "ada", "password": "…"}'           # returns data.csrf_token
curl -s -b jar "http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/people?q=circadian"
curl -s -b jar -H "X-CSRF-Token: <csrf_token>" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -X POST "http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/assertions/asr_…/decision" -d '{"action": "accept"}'

Every change needs the session's CSRF token in X-CSRF-Token, and each route needs a role: reading needs any account, deciding needs a curator for that person (or the person themselves, as a researcher), and managing accounts needs an admin. A missing session is a 401, a missing role or scope a 403.

Conventions#

  • Envelopes. A successful response is {"data": …, "meta": {…}}. Lists put paging details and facet counts in meta.
  • Paging. Lists take limit (up to 200) and either offset or the opaque cursor from the previous page's meta.next_cursor, which is null on the last page.
  • Sparse fields. fields=id,name returns only those keys of each list item.
  • Errors are RFC 9457 problem details (application/problem+json) with a title, a status, and a plain-language detail.
  • Search highlights in highlights mark matches with the private-use characters U+E000 and U+E001, never HTML. Escape the text, then replace those characters with your own markup.
  • Identifiers. People have IDs such as per_01…. Records use their public identifiers: a PubMed ID, a grant's core project number (for example R01CA123456), or an NCT number.

SystemGET /health, GET /meta
Search and overviewGET /search (everything, or one kind with facets), GET /stats, GET /sources, GET /runs
PeopleGET /people, GET /people/{id}, and the person's /publications, /grants, /studies, and /history; POST /people/{id}/publications adds a paper by PubMed ID; POST /people/{id}/identifiers adds, confirms, rejects, or removes an identifier
RecordsGET /publications, /grants, /studies, each with search and facets, and one record by its identifier with its connections and provenance
OpportunitiesGET /opportunities (search and facets: status, source, kind, closing, agency, activity, category; sorted by closing date, soonest first), GET /opportunities/{id} (Grants.gov's numeric ID, or opl_… for the organization's own); curators for everyone and admins: POST /opportunities (add one the organization tracks), POST /opportunities/{id} (change it, or restore it with archived: false), DELETE /opportunities/{id} (archive it), POST /opportunities/import (rows, or csv text with a header; source, complete, dry_run). The list also filters by eligible (meets, check, does_not_meet), fits (a person's ID, or me), and group; GET /opportunities/{id}/fit (who fits and why, with concept and drop to adjust the reading, and the eligibility checks), POST /opportunities/{id}/interest (watching, dismissed, or null, for yourself), GET /me/opportunities (those that fit your work, and those you watch), GET /people/{id}/opportunities, GET /groups/{group}/opportunities

CurationGET /assertions/{id} (a link with its evidence and history), POST /assertions/{id}/decision, POST /assertions/decisions (one decision for many), GET /review (the queue), POST /review/bulk, GET /decisions (the log)
Discovered peopleGET /candidates, POST /candidates/decisions (promote, dismiss, or undo)

Sessions and accountsGET /session (who is signed in), POST /session (sign in), DELETE /session (sign out), POST /session/password; admins: GET /accounts, POST /accounts, POST /accounts/{id}

OperationsAdmins: GET /operations (every source and job over 30 days with failures, durations, and the last error; recent runs; sizes; the last backup; and what needs attention)
Saved searchesEach person's own: GET /saved-searches (with how many matches are new), POST /saved-searches (name, kind, q, filters), GET /saved-searches/{id} (with the new matches), POST /saved-searches/{id} (rename), POST /saved-searches/{id}/seen, DELETE /saved-searches/{id}
Duplicates and mergesGET /duplicates (possible duplicate pairs with evidence), POST /merges (merge two records), POST /merges/{id}/undo
Graph viewsGET /graph/network (people linked by collaboration and, where they have none, by similarity; unit, max_people, min_similarity, similar), GET /graph/focus/{id} (a person, their top concepts, and who shares them, with their collaborators), GET /graph/landscape (concepts linked by the people who hold them together). Each returns {"nodes": [...], "edges": [...]}; the network's meta also names the collaboration groups and the people who bridge them
Missed connections and unitsGET /graph/missed (alike, current, and never worked together; min_similarity, unit, across, paging), GET /graph/units (unit-by-unit interest and collaboration densities), GET /graph/units/pairs?a=&b= (the pairs behind one cell)
DiscoveryGET /experts (people ranked by matching concepts, from words q or descriptors concept, with the concepts the query became in meta.concepts), GET /concepts?q= (descriptors by name), GET /people/{id}/similar (similar people with shared concepts, and whether they have worked together), GET /people/{id}/concepts (what drives a profile, with the person's research areas in meta.areas)

All paths are under /api/v1.

Examples#

Find people who work on a topic, with the concepts that match:

curl -s "http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/experts?q=circadian+rhythms&limit=10"

Or start from a MeSH descriptor (here, Neoplasms) and find who shares it:

curl -s "http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/experts?concept=D009369"

The organization's active grants, largest first:

curl -s "http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/grants?status=active&sort=amount&limit=10"

Why Tessera links a person to a paper, and how to accept it:

curl -s "http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/assertions/asr_…"
curl -s -X POST "http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/assertions/asr_…/decision" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" -d '{"action": "accept", "note": "Checked the author list"}'

Decisions made through the API are recorded under the signed-in person's name (or, without accounts, the [server] operator name), exactly like decisions in the web app.
