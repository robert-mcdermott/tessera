Open research intelligence for academic research organizations

Every contribution, connected.

Tessera builds your organization's research footprint from public data: people, publications, grants, and clinical studies, linked with the evidence behind every connection. Curators confirm links in seconds, researchers keep profiles that build their CVs and NIH biosketches, and everyone can find who works on what, who should meet, what followed the work, and which funding fits it next.

Starts from public data

PubMed, NIH RePORTER, ClinicalTrials.gov, iCite, ORCID, MeSH, and Grants.gov. No HR feeds or integrations to begin.

Evidence on every link

Each connection records its source, the evidence for and against it, and who decided.

Humans decide

Automation suggests and curators confirm; decisions survive every refresh and can be undone.

Open and yours

Apache 2.0, runs on a laptop or one server, keeps data in open formats, and takes your brand.

Explainable suggestions

See why, every time

Every suggested link between a person and a paper comes with its evidence, weighed in plain language: the ORCID iD on the paper, the organization in the affiliation, a grant it acknowledges, shared coauthors, a different first name. Weights can be learned from your own decisions, and auto-accept switches on only where the measured precision allows.

A home for every lab and program

Their own managers arrange it from approved blocks and publish it, with drafts, history, and rollback, and a weekly inbox brings them what changed.

Profiles

A profile they keep

Beside what Tessera builds from public records, every researcher keeps their own sections: an overview and interests, earlier positions, education, honors, service, teaching, talks, links, and the papers they want to feature. They edit them in place, or name someone to do it for them, and every change is recorded. Everyone inside sees the whole profile; the public sees only what the researcher chooses, section by section.

CVs and biosketches

A CV that stays current, a biosketch NIH accepts

Keep named CVs, one for a promotion and one for a visiting post, each with its own sections and order, built from the profile and confirmed records whenever it is opened, then print or save it as PDF or download it. NIH biosketches follow the form NIH requires since January 2026, with its limits checked as you write and each section ready to paste into SciENcv. With the assistant on, it can draft a first personal statement from the papers you choose.

Portfolio

The portfolio, honestly

Publications, funding, topics, and studies for the organization, a division, a program, a lab, or people you pick, over the years you choose. Impact

What followed the work

Every grant shows what came after it: the papers that acknowledge it, the registered studies that reference them, and the clinical articles that cite them, with practice guidelines marked and the years from award to first clinical citation. Each step says how it is linked and where that came from, and only confirmed links are followed. It shows association, never causation.

Funding

Funding that fits

Federal opportunities from Grants.gov, and your own foundation calls, pilot funds, and limited submissions, each read as topics and matched to the people and groups whose confirmed work fits it, with the reasons shown. Start a team from an opportunity to see which topics its members cover, who could fill the gaps, and a brief to share.

Optional assistant

Ask, and see the sources

Turn on the assistant and ask in plain words: who works on this, what followed that grant, which opportunities fit this lab. It looks things up with read-only tools, as the person asking, so it sees only what they may see, and every answer cites the records it rests on. A citation it cannot stand behind is removed, and a question the records do not answer is declined, never guessed. It works with any OpenAI-compatible or Anthropic-compatible endpoint, official, self-hosted, or a model on your own machine, and with Amazon Bedrock. It is off until you turn it on, and every exchange is logged.

Plain language

Summaries people approve

With the assistant on, a profile or a group can have a short summary of its work for patients, donors, and students, drafted from confirmed records only. Nobody sees a draft but its reviewers: the person, their curators, or the group's managers, who edit, approve, or reject it. Approved summaries are labeled as drafted with AI assistance, and by whom they were approved.

How it works

Connectors fetch public records into a Parquet lake. The evidence engine builds the directory and suggests links, each with its evidence; curators decide in the ledger; and the search index and discovery data feed a JSON API and a fast, accessible web app.

Try it in a minute

Tessera needs uv, which provides Python 3.12 or newer. The demo builds an invented research institute, with about 100 people, their publications, grants, studies, and a curator's decisions:

git clone https://github.com/robert-mcdermott/tessera.git
cd tessera
uv sync
uv run tessera demo
uv run tessera --config demo/tessera.toml serve

Then open http://127.0.0.1:8000. To try the assistant there too, see Trying it on the demo. To set Tessera up for your own organization, start with Getting started.
