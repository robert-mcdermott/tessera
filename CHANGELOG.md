# Changelog

All notable changes to Tessera are recorded here. Versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org/); each wave of work ships as a minor release (wave 1 → 0.1.0, wave 2 → 0.2.0).

## Unreleased

### Added

- Profile sections a researcher keeps (PRF-02, PRF-06): an overview, research interests, positions and appointments, education and training, honors and awards, memberships and service, teaching and mentoring, talks and presentations, links, and featured publications (their confirmed papers only, up to ten), each with its own fields and checks (migration 0019). Everyone signed in reads every profile; the person, a curator for them, and an admin change it, and every change is recorded with who made it, in what capacity, and the entry before and after. Each entry records where it came from, so an import could later find its own entries again instead of adding copies, and never touch those made by hand. `GET /people/{id}/profile` and its routes. The demo fills in a dozen lab heads' profiles.
- Profiles open on **About** (PRF-01): the overview and research interests, positions (the group directory's, then the researcher's own), education, honors, service, teaching, talks, links, and featured publications. Those who keep the profile add, change, reorder, and remove entries in place; everyone else reads the same page, with empty sections hidden. The **History** tab lists profile changes beside curation decisions.
- Someone to act for them (PRF-05): a researcher, a curator for them, or an admin names accounts that keep the profile on the person's behalf, such as an assistant (migration 0020). They change the profile, and later its CVs and biosketches, but not the person's papers, identifiers, or who else keeps it; every change they make is recorded as made for the person, and the profile says who keeps it. The profiles an account keeps are in its own menu.
- Public by choice (PRF-03): the researcher, or someone acting for them, turns their public page on and off (curators and admins still can), chooses which sections it shows (their own sections, and groups, publications, grants, studies, and topics), and leaves single entries off it (migration 0021). The public site shows exactly those, in the profile's order; inside, everything is always shown. **Preview the public page** shows it as the next publish would build it.
- CVs (PRF-04): a profile's keepers start CVs from its **About** tab and keep several named versions (migration 0022). Each chooses its sections (positions, including the group directory's, education, honors, service, teaching, talks, publications, grants, studies, and a summary), their order, and their entries: all of them, including any added later, or a chosen list in a chosen order; publications can be all confirmed papers, those since a year, or chosen ones, numbered or not. A CV is built from the profile and confirmed records each time it is shown, cited close to the National Library of Medicine's style with the person's name in bold, and prints or saves as PDF without the app around it, or downloads as Markdown. CVs are seen by the profile's keepers only.
- NIH biosketches (PRF-04): **New NIH biosketch** on a profile prepares NIH's current form (the Biographical Sketch Common Form and NIH Biographical Sketch Supplement, for applications due on or after January 25, 2026): identifying information with the ORCID iD, professional preparation from education, appointments and positions, up to five products closely related to the project and five other significant ones (confirmed papers or another product's citation), the personal statement, up to fifteen honors, and up to five contributions to science. NIH's limits are checked as one writes (3,500 characters for the statement, 2,000 for each contribution, no citations in either), each problem said with what is over and by how much. **Copy for SciENcv** gives each section as text to paste, since NIH requires the investigator to complete and certify the forms there; it also prints and downloads as Markdown. With the assistant on, **Draft with the assistant** writes a first personal statement or contribution from papers the researcher chooses, which they can use and edit; with it off, nothing mentions it.

## 0.5.0 (2026-11-08)

Impact. Tessera now follows what came after a grant or a paper, finds the funding opportunities that fit people and groups and says separately whether the organization may apply, and helps put a team together for one. For organizations that turn it on, an assistant answers questions from Tessera's own records with citations, within each person's access, and drafts plain-language summaries that someone approves before anyone sees them.

**Highlights:** grant-to-impact threads (papers, studies, and clinical articles, with practice guidelines marked) and Insight → Impact; Grants.gov's federal opportunities and the organization's own; fit and eligibility as separate signals, each with its reasons, on the home page, profiles, group pages, and in a group's inbox; teams with the topics they cover and miss, and a brief; an optional assistant for any OpenAI-compatible or Anthropic-compatible endpoint or Amazon Bedrock, invisible until enabled; grounded answers that cite records and decline what they do not support; plain-language summaries for profiles and group pages.

**Upgrading:** run `tessera db upgrade` (migrations 0014 to 0018), then `tessera sync`, which now also fetches Grants.gov's opportunities (the first time takes about 12 minutes; later syncs read only what changed) and iCite's clinical citations with the citing articles. Set `[organization] applicant_types` for the eligibility checks (see Configuration). The assistant stays off, and nothing mentions it, until an `[ai]` section turns it on; see *The assistant*.

**Checks for this release:** authorship is unchanged: on the pilot, with people's ORCID works lists as they are today, 0.5.0 and the 0.4.0 ledger give the same result (196 listed papers from 2025 on, 94.9% found as candidates, 97.3% of those strong; the lists have grown by three papers since 0.4.0's check of 193). A grant's thread answers in 54 ms at most on the pilot's data; its first Grants.gov sync took 12 minutes 15 seconds and an unchanged one 1.9 seconds; 1,459 open opportunities are read for fit in 100 seconds. The assistant was evaluated on the demo with `gemma4:31b-cloud` through Ollama: of 35 questions, every answer's citations were valid before checking, every answerable question cited an expected record, all 7 unanswerable questions were declined, and nothing out of the asker's reach was ever sent to the model. Migrations 0014 to 0018 were run up and down on SQLite and PostgreSQL 16. No dependencies were added.


### Added

- Grant-to-impact threads (INT-08): every grant's page shows what followed it, in typed and sourced steps: the papers that acknowledge it, the registered studies that reference those papers or name the grant, and the clinical articles that cite the papers, with practice guidelines and trials marked and the years between award and first clinical citation. Choosing a paper, study, or article shows only what it is linked to; **Paths to practice guidelines** picks those out. Papers list the clinical articles citing them; people have an **Impact** tab. Only accepted links that their source still reports are followed, and threads say they show association, not causation. `GET /grants/{core}/thread`, `GET /publications/{pmid}/thread`, `GET /people/{id}/impact`.
- iCite's clinical citations (`cited_by_clin`) are kept (lake table `icite_clinical_citation`, filled as each paper's metrics are refreshed (monthly), or at once with `tessera ingest icite --full`), and `tessera sync` then fetches the citing articles' PubMed records (`pubmed:citing`) into their own table, `citing_article`, so they never become candidates for anyone's authorship.
- **Insight → Impact**: `impact.clinical_citations/v1` (a cohort's papers cited by clinical articles and by practice guidelines, by year of publication) and `impact.studies/v1` (its papers listed by registered studies), with their caveats; both can be saved in reports, snapshotted, and exported.
- The demo organization's papers are cited by invented clinical articles, practice guidelines among them.
- Funding opportunities (INT-09): Grants.gov's forecast and posted federal opportunities, from its public search and opportunity APIs (no key), in a new source, `grants_gov` (`[sources.grants_gov]`, with optional `categories` and `agencies`). Each sync reads only what changed; NIH notices carry their activity codes, clinical-trial requirement, and NIH Guide link. Agency contact details are not kept. Lake table `grants_gov_opportunity`.
- Opportunities an organization tracks itself (INT-12): foundation calls, internal pilot funds, and limited submissions, kept in the ledger (migration 0014), added and edited in **Admin → Opportunities**, or imported from CSV or JSON with `tessera opportunities import` (or `POST /opportunities/import`); importing again updates in place, and nothing is written when any row has a problem.
- **Opportunities** in the menu: every opportunity, open and forecast first by closing date, with search and filters (status, closing within 30 or 90 days, source, kind, sponsor, activity code, category), an opportunity's page with its dates, money, who may apply, and details, and opportunities in search, the command palette, and saved searches.
- The demo organization has twelve invented federal opportunities and three of its own.
- The opportunity navigator (INT-09): each open or forecast opportunity is read as topics, the way an expert search reads a question, and matched to the people whose confirmed work fits it (**strong** or **some** fit, with the headings that match) and the groups that fit through their members. On an opportunity's page, **Who fits** shows them and the topics it was read as, which can be removed or added to read it again. **Can the organization apply?** checks, separately, the applicant type against the new `[organization] applicant_types`, the deadline, an internal deadline, limited submission, career stage, and cost sharing, each with what it was decided from; clinical-trial rules and forecast dates are notes. The list filters by eligibility and **Fits my work**; the home page shows opportunities that fit your work, profiles and group pages show theirs, and a group's inbox and digest list new ones that fit it. **Watch** and **Dismiss** are your own (migration 0015). Fits are worked out after each sync, or by `tessera build opportunities`. A guide, *Opportunities*, describes it all.
- Teams (INT-10): **Start a team** on an opportunity, or start one for topics you choose (**Teams** in the menu). A team's page shows which of the topics it needs each member covers, from their confirmed work; the missing topics, each with people who cover it and their reasons; others who fit the opportunity; who has worked together and which pairings are new; and the groups it spans. Members take roles. A team is seen by its owner, its members, the people it is shared with, and admins (migration 0016). **Team brief** prints or saves as PDF, or downloads as Markdown (members as CSV), with each member's confirmed papers on its topics, active grants, and studies.
- The optional assistant's foundation (AI-01, AI-02, AI-05, AI-07): an `[ai]` section, off unless enabled, for any endpoint that speaks OpenAI's Chat Completions API or Anthropic's Messages API (official, self-hosted, or local, such as Ollama) or Amazon Bedrock's Converse API, all spoken over plain HTTP with streaming and tool calls (ADR-0010). Bedrock takes an Amazon Bedrock API key or access keys from the environment, signed with Signature Version 4 from the standard library. Keys are only ever read from the environment; one written in the configuration is refused. While it is off, its routes, API document entries, menu items, and files do not exist. Everything a model is sent passes through a filter that removes contact details and links nobody has accepted. Every exchange is logged (migration 0017) and shown to admins in **Admin → Assistant**; `tessera ai check` tries the connection. The example configuration (and so a new `tessera.toml`) ends with the `[ai]` section commented out, every setting listed. A guide, *The assistant*.
- Grounded questions and answers (AI-03, AI-08): with the assistant on, **Ask** in the top bar and the command palette opens a panel where answers stream in with numbered citations and the records they rest on, follow-up questions keep the conversation, and **Stop** ends an answer. The model looks things up with read-only tools (search; open a record; experts for a topic, with their papers on it; a person's or group's work; an Insight figure, counting confirmed records only; an impact thread; an opportunity's fit; opportunities for a person or group), each of which asks Tessera's own API with the asker's session, so it sees what they may see and nothing else. A citation is kept only if a tool returned that record while answering, and an answer left with nothing to cite becomes a decline that says what was searched; text is held back until it cites a record, so such an answer never appears. Record text reaches the model only as tool results. The instructions are versioned (`assistant/v1`). `POST /api/v1/ai/ask` streams JSON lines; the daily limit and `[ai] roles` apply.
- Plain-language summaries (AI-04, AI-06): with the assistant on, a profile's reviewers (the person, a curator for them, an admin) or a group's managers and curators can draft a short summary for a general reader from confirmed records only, then edit, approve, or reject it; nobody else sees a draft. Approved summaries show on the profile and in a new **Summary** group page block, labeled as drafted with AI assistance and approved by whom, and on the public site for public people and groups. An approved summary is replaced only by approving another, and when the records have changed a good deal the group's inbox (or the person's own) suggests a new draft. Each draft keeps its model, instructions (`summary/v1`), and a digest of its facts, and every change is recorded (migration 0018). With the assistant off, approved summaries are kept but shown nowhere.

### Changed

- Curation and administration pages load when first opened, not with the app, which keeps the app's first load smaller.
- An expert search reads plural words as singular, so "germinal centers" finds Germinal Center.

## 0.4.0 (2026-11-08)

Insight. Leaders can now see what the organization, a division, a program, or a lab publishes, is funded for, works on, and runs as studies, with every number saying exactly what it counts. Funding is counted the way a sponsored-research office counts it, small numbers are marked, trends are claimed only when the numbers support them, and nothing ranks people. Reports can be frozen as snapshots that verify byte for byte years later, exports carry their definitions and the records behind them, and the whole installation can be backed up, restored (into PostgreSQL too), watched, and left.

**Highlights:** metric definitions with versions and an About panel on every figure; cohorts of the organization, a group (counting members' work while they were members), picked people, or a search; the funding portfolio with strict counting rules and grants by project end date; topics and trends; the study portfolio by registry status, phase, sponsor, intervention, and condition; field-normalized citation; saved searches with new matches; reports, verifiable snapshots, and evidence-carrying exports; backup, restore, and a full exit export; Admin → Operations and alerts; and embeds of group content in other websites.

**Upgrading:** run `tessera db upgrade` (migrations 0012 and 0013), then take a first backup with `tessera backup` and schedule it (see Running Tessera). To have problems emailed, set `[alerts] email` with a `[mail]` server. Embeds need `[publish] base_url` for their code to be shown.

**Checks for this release:** authorship is unchanged; on the pilot, the check against people's own ORCID works lists gives the same result as 0.3.0 (95.3% of 193 listed papers found as candidates, 97.3% of those in the strong band). Every Insight figure answers in 160 ms or less on the pilot's data, and the operations view in 29 ms. A demo backup restored into a new data home gives identical ledger rows and API answers, and a SQLite backup restores into PostgreSQL 16 with identical rows. Migrations 0012 and 0013 were run up and down on SQLite and PostgreSQL. No dependencies were added.

### Added

- Insight (wave 4): portfolio figures that say exactly what they count. Every figure is a metric over a cohort and a period, and **About** shows the metric's definition and version (`publications.count/v1`), the cohort and its size, the period, the options, the sources with when each was last fetched, notes, and the records counted with the links behind each. **Insight → Definitions** lists every metric. Counts above zero and below `[insight] small_number` (5) are hatched in charts and marked in tables; years before a source's first sync reached are marked incomplete, and the default period starts where the data is complete. No figure ranks people.
- Cohorts: the organization, a group (with or without the groups beneath it, counting each member's work while they were a member where the dates are known, or everything its current members have done), picked people, or a people search. Cohorts can be saved under a name for everyone (`/api/v1/insight/cohorts`, ledger migration 0012).
- The first figure: publications by year, confirmed and likely, with *confirmed only* one click away.
- **Insight → Funding** (INT-02, INT-05): awards by fiscal year (new, renewal, continuing, and supplement), by funding institute, by activity code, by mechanism, and by group, in total cost, direct costs, or indirect costs, always labeled; and grants by project end date over the next 6, 12, or 24 months, labeled as end dates rather than funding gaps. Money is counted per fiscal-year record with subprojects left out, so a center grant's cores are not counted twice, and a grant counts once however many members are on it. The organization's portfolio is every award to it; a cohort's is its members' awards, to the organization or anywhere.
- Embeds (LAB-09): `tessera publish` writes, for every public group, pages made to be framed in another website (its publications, people, openings, and news, each following the reader's light or dark preference or held to one) and a JSON Feed of its news, openings, and publications. **Edit page → Embed on another website** gives the code once `[publish] base_url` is set. Embeds carry no scripts and show only what the group's public page shows.
- The demo organization's public site has an address (`https://research.corvane.example`, a name that cannot exist), so its sitemap, canonical links, and embed codes are shown.
- Backup and restore (PLT-08): `tessera backup` writes one archive of the ledger (as portable rows), the lake, report snapshots, and the configuration, with a SHA-256 digest for every file, under the job lock; `--verify` checks one, and `--raw` adds the raw responses. `tessera restore` loads an archive into an empty data home and ledger, at the backup's schema and then upgraded, so a backup from SQLite restores into PostgreSQL and from an older release into a newer one.
- The full exit export (PLT-08): `tessera export <folder>` writes every ledger and lake table as Parquet and CSV, report snapshots, a data dictionary, and a manifest with digests, readable without Tessera. Password hashes and sessions are left out.
- **Admin → Operations** (PLT-12): what needs attention, every source and job over the last 30 days (runs, failures, typical and recent run times, last error), recent runs, storage by part of the data home and lake table, the last backup, and whether a job is running; `GET /api/v1/operations`.
- Alerts (PLT-12): `[alerts] email` hears about failed runs, stale sources, and old backups, once per new set of issues, at the end of `tessera sync` or from `tessera alerts --send`. `tessera doctor` warns when the last backup is older than `[alerts] backup_days`.
- Reports and snapshots (INT-03): **Save as report** on any Insight page saves its figures with the cohort, years, and options; **Insight → Reports** lists them. A snapshot freezes a report with the exact inputs its figures were computed from (Parquet), the figures with their records, and a manifest with digests, in `snapshots/<id>/` in the data home; **Verify** computes every figure again from the frozen inputs and confirms each is identical. Later syncs never change a snapshot. `tessera report list`, `snapshot`, `verify`, and `export`. Ledger migration 0013.
- Exports (INT-04): every figure exports as CSV or Parquet (a zip with the figure, the records counted with their links, `about.json`, and `README.txt`) or JSON, carrying the metric's definition and version, the cohort, the period, the options and filters, and the sources with their data timestamps; Parquet files also carry it in their metadata. A snapshot exports all its figures with its manifest.
- Saved searches (DIS-10): **Save search** on People, Publications, Grants, Studies, and Search; the home page lists your saved searches with how many matches are new since you last looked, and opening one shows them, with **Mark as seen**.
- **Insight → Topics** (INT-06): the cohort's papers by major MeSH heading or rolled up to research areas, with each topic's share and trend, and the twelve largest year by year. A topic is called rising or falling only when it has enough papers in both halves of the period's complete years; otherwise the figure says there are too few papers, or too few years, to tell.
- **Insight → Studies** (INT-07): studies under way in the period by registry status, phase, lead sponsor class, intervention type, condition (as disease areas or as registered), and start year. Status is always labeled as the registry's, never as local recruitment.
- Field-normalized citation: the distribution of iCite's relative citation ratio for the cohort's papers, with the last two years' papers kept apart because their ratios are provisional.
- The demo organization's studies vary in sponsor class (industry, NIH, a network), intervention type, and design (one is observational).
- The demo organization gains a center grant whose cores report as subprojects, a supplement, an institutional training grant, and a career award, and its research grants carry RePORTER's mechanism names.

### Fixed

- A grant's latest award on its page and in search is now its own record for the latest fiscal year, never one of its subprojects' records.

## 0.3.0 (2026-11-08)

Showcase. Every lab, program, and division now has a home that its own people keep: members with roles and dates, the papers that are truly the group's, its grants, studies, themes, openings, and news, on a page its managers arrange and publish. A weekly inbox brings them what changed, a public site shows the groups and people who opt in, and MeSH teaches expert search synonyms and the hierarchy of topics.

**Highlights:** groups of any configured kind, nested under as many parents as they span, curated by hand or imported from your own records with their identifiers; lab attribution as its own decision; group pages built from approved blocks with drafts, previews, versions, and rollback; a manager role limited to a group; the inbox and email digests; openings that close themselves; themes and projects; a static, checked, allowlisted public site; the brand kit with WCAG AA checks; and MeSH synonyms, narrower headings, and research areas.

**Upgrading:** run `tessera db upgrade` (migrations 0007 to 0011). Existing units become groups that came from the roster, each with an address; existing memberships become plain members. Nobody appears on the public site until they opt in. The collaboration graph's "groups" are now called clusters.

**Checks for this release:** authorship is unchanged; on the pilot, the check against people's own ORCID works lists gives the same result as 0.2.0 (95.3% of 193 listed papers found as candidates, 97.3% of those in the strong band). The new group endpoints answer in 15 ms or less at the 95th percentile on the demo (the digest across 31 groups in 29 ms), and the demo's 103-page public site builds and passes its checks in about a second. Migrations 0007 to 0011 were run up and down on SQLite and PostgreSQL with data in place. No dependencies were added.

### Added

- Groups: divisions, departments, programs, centers, shared resources, labs, and any other kind you configure (`[vocabulary]` names the kinds, the membership roles, and what the app calls a group). A group can sit under several others, has an address that survives renames, and can be archived or merged into another. Memberships carry a role as well as a title and dates; ending one keeps the person as an alumnus, and removing one is for mistakes. **Admin → Groups** curates all of it, with each group's history. `tessera groups import` (and `POST /api/v1/groups/import`) reads groups and memberships from CSV or JSON with the source's own identifiers, so importing the same source again updates instead of duplicating; `--complete` archives what the source no longer lists and `--dry-run` changes nothing. The roster import goes through the same path and can give a group's kind and a person's role. A curator limited to a group reaches the groups beneath it through any parent. Ledger migration 0007.
- Group pages: **Groups** lists every division, program, lab, and shared resource, and each has a page with what it does, its leaders and contact, its people and alumni, the publications attributed to it, its members' grants and studies, its topics and broader areas, and the groups it writes most with. Profiles list a person's groups with their roles and link to them.
- Lab attribution: whether a paper is a group's own is a link of its own (`attributed_to`), suggested at each sync for labs, programs, centers, shared resources, and teams (`[vocabulary] attributable_kinds`) with its evidence (member authors, a leader as last author, an affiliation naming the group, a member's grant, the group's topics), and always decided by a person on the group's page. A leader's authorship alone is weak evidence. `tessera groups attribute` refreshes the suggestions. Groups gain a contact address and a website (migration 0008).
- Managers: a role limited to one group (`manager=group:<address>`, or `+below` for the groups beneath it too) for the person who keeps the group's page and decides which papers are its own. Admin → Accounts offers it with a group picker.
- Each group's page is made of blocks from an approved set (about, people, publications with featured papers, grants, studies, topics, writes most with, themes and projects, openings, contact, news), arranged in **Edit page** without code, saved as a draft, previewed, and published as numbered versions that can be viewed and restored. Migration 0009.
- Openings: jobs and training a group posts, each closing by itself on its date (or early, or extended).
- Themes and projects: a group's own account of what it works on, linking people, grants, publications, and studies.
- The inbox: each group's weekly review in one place for its managers and curators (papers to attribute, new grants, roster changes, openings closing soon), with **Mark as reviewed**, and a count in the menu. `tessera digest send` emails each person their pending work once a week through `[mail]`; `tessera digest show` prints it. Migration 0010.
- The public site: `tessera publish` writes static pages for the groups and people who opt in (**Public page** and **Public profile** in the app, or `tessera groups public` and `tessera people public`), from an allowlisted projection: citations and links, never abstracts; confirmed papers only; no one's email address. Each build is checked (one language, title, and main heading per page, headings in order, landmarks, text alternatives, no scripts or inline styles, no broken links, WCAG AA colors in light and dark) before it replaces the published site. Sitemap, canonical links, Open Graph tags, and schema.org data when `[publish] base_url` is set. Migration 0011.
- The brand kit: `[branding.palette.day]` and `.night` set the organization's colors (page, cards, text, borders, accent and its text, a stripe, and each record type), for the app and the public site; `favicon`, `social_image` (link previews of the public site), and `footer_links` (privacy, accessibility, and the like, in the app's sidebar and on every public page). `tessera doctor` checks every color pair against WCAG AA by day and by night and fails any below it.
- The demo organization has labs, programs that span divisions, and a shared resource, imported from its registry, with alumni and decided attributions, and it publishes its own public site.
- MeSH, the National Library of Medicine's vocabulary, as a source: `tessera sync` reads the annual descriptor file once a year (`[sources.mesh]`). Expert search understands synonyms ("cancer" is Neoplasms, "AML" is acute myeloid leukemia), shows which synonym a concept came from, and lets a broad heading include the narrower ones beneath it; concept search finds headings by synonym; profiles show research areas rolled up the MeSH hierarchy; and the concept landscape is colored by MeSH category. NLM is credited, with the MeSH year, wherever these appear. New lake table `mesh_descriptor`; `derived/concept_vocabulary.parquet` and `derived/concept_area.parquet`.

- `tessera demo [PATH]` builds a complete installation for an invented organization, the Corvane Institute for Biomedical Research: about 100 people in 22 labs, 700 publications, grants, clinical studies, ORCID records, and a curator's decisions, so every screen has something to show. Identifiers are chosen so they can never be mistaken for real ones. It is also what the documentation's screenshots are taken from.
- Screenshots for the documentation are taken from the real app, serving the demo organization, in light and dark (`node docs/capture/capture.mjs`), and the diagrams are drawn as SVG (`node docs/capture/diagrams.mjs`).
- A product home page for the documentation site, figures in the guides, a social preview card, and a screenshot at the top of the README.
- A documentation site, built from the guides with `node docs/site/build.mjs` (Node.js 22, no packages): a menu in reading order, search, light and dark themes from Tessera's own design tokens, an API reference generated from the OpenAPI document, and a check that fails the build on any broken link or anchor.

### Changed

- The collaboration graph's communities are now called clusters, so they are not confused with the organization's groups.
- Units are now groups. The API's person `units` entries add the membership's role, the group's kind and address; `GET /people?unit=` and the unit matrix list only active groups.
- In the person focus view, the person in focus stays at the centre, and topics are drawn larger than the people around them, so people gather around the topics they share.

### Fixed

- With reduced motion turned on, the graphs drew every node and label in black: a tiny transition on every element made the colors read back wrong. Reduced motion now turns transitions off.
- The collaboration graph's legend lists former members only when the graph shows one.

## 0.2.0 (2026-11-08)

Connections. Tessera now helps people find who works on what and who should meet, with the reasons on screen, and lets several people curate with their own accounts and roles. ORCID strengthens the evidence, duplicate records merge reversibly, and the evidence model can learn from local decisions.

**Highlights:** ORCID records and iDs as evidence; possible duplicates and reversible merges; dated unit memberships; local accounts with admin, curator (for everyone, a unit, or a person), researcher, and viewer roles; learned weights evaluated on held-out people, and auto-accept only where the measured precision is at least 99%; the expert finder; concept profiles that show what drives them; similar people; collaboration, person focus, and concept landscape graphs with list views; missed connections; groups and the people who bridge them; and the unit matrix.

### Evaluation

- **Pilot check against people's own ORCID works lists:** Tessera reads an ORCID record only once the iD is confirmed, and none was, so the records behind the 49 suggested iDs are independent of every score. Of 193 papers that 39 people list themselves (within the pilot's two-year window), 95.3% were found as candidates (target 95%); of those, 97.3% are in the strong band and all are at or above the review threshold. Works lists measure recall, not precision: people list only some of their papers. Details are in [spec/evidence-engine.md](spec/evidence-engine.md#pilot-check-against-orcid-works-lists-v020).
- **Learned weights and auto-accept** need curator decisions, which the pilot does not have yet. On generated decisions, fitting recovers the weights that produced them, and a learned model is offered only when it predicts better on people left out of the fit. On the synthetic organization with simulated decisions, auto-accept at 0.99 accepted 477 suggestions, all correct, and undoing the batch restored every one.
- **Discovery on the synthetic organization:** every person's top three concepts come from their own field, searching a field's heading returns that field's people first, at least 90% of similar pairs and of collaborating pairs share a field, and collaboration counts match an independent recount exactly.
- **Speed on the pilot:** the expert finder answers in 31 ms at the 95th percentile for a query in words (13 ms for a chosen concept); similar people, a profile's concepts, and every graph view in under 10 ms. Graphs of about 150 nodes, and a test graph of 300 nodes and 600 links, lay out with no main-thread task over 50 ms.

### Added

- Missed connections (DIS-08): pairs of current members whose interests are alike but who have never published, held a grant, or run a study together, with the topics they share and anyone who has worked with both. Filters for unit, pairs across units, and how alike. Profiles' **Similar people** now say whether each pair has worked together. `GET /api/v1/graph/missed`.
- Groups and bridges: the collaboration network is divided into groups by modularity, each named by its members' most distinctive topics and given its own colour; people who connect groups are ringed and listed by name with the groups they work with. `derived/person_group.parquet`.
- The unit matrix (DIS-09): every pair of units compared by interest (shade) and collaboration (inner square), each per possible pair of members; each cell opens the people behind it, the matrix works from the keyboard, and a sortable list has the same information. `GET /api/v1/graph/units`, `/graph/units/pairs`.
- Categorical colours (`--series-0..5`) in every palette, tested to stay distinct from one another and visible against cards in light and dark.
- Connections (DIS-07): three graph views under Explore, beside **Missed connections** and **Units**. **Collaboration** shows people linked by shared papers, grants, and studies (weighted so that one large consortium paper does not outweigh a long partnership) and, dashed, by similar interests where they have no shared work; **Person focus** shows someone's top topics and the people who share them, with a trail to step back; **Concept landscape** shows research areas as topics linked by the people who hold them together. Hover to see someone's connections, select for details, double-click to focus, drag to pin, search to fly to a name; every view has a sortable **List** view with the same information, and its state is in the URL. The layout runs in a Web Worker and the views load on first use. `GET /api/v1/graph/network`, `/graph/focus/{id}`, `/graph/landscape`; `derived/collaboration_edge.parquet` and `derived/concept_edge.parquet`.
- Find experts (DIS-05): describe a topic in your own words and Tessera ranks people by the topics of their papers, with the concepts that match on every result. Words are read through the organization's papers that they find, so "cancer" reaches the people whose papers are indexed under "Neoplasms"; the concepts a search became are shown as **Understood as**, where you can drop one or add a heading by name. `GET /api/v1/experts`, `GET /api/v1/concepts`.
- Concept profiles (DIS-04): every directory member's topics as weighted MeSH headings from their papers, counting major topics, first and last authorship, and recent work more, and headings many people share less. Strong suggestions count half until confirmed (`[discovery] likely_weight`); author keywords stand in, marked, for papers not yet indexed; studies add their conditions and interventions. **How this is built** on a profile's Topics card shows each concept's weight, papers, and confirmed papers. Built by `tessera sync` or `tessera build discovery` (which also builds the graphs) into `derived/concept_profile.parquet`; `GET /api/v1/people/{id}/concepts`.
- Similar people (DIS-06): each profile lists the people whose topics overlap most, with the shared concepts, precomputed into `derived/similarity_pair.parquet`. `GET /api/v1/people/{id}/similar`.
- A `[discovery]` section for the recency half-life, the weight of unconfirmed suggestions, and the similarity floor; a [Finding experts](docs/discovery.md) guide.
- Learned weights (`authorship/v2`, CUR-10): Tessera fits one multiplier per kind of evidence, and an offset, to curators' accept and reject decisions, pulled toward the hand-set weights so a few decisions barely move it. It is evaluated on people left out of the fit and offered only if it predicts better there (at least 200 decisions, 20 rejections, 5 people). Admins release or revert it under **Admin → Evidence** or with `tessera evidence`; explanations keep both weights.
- Safe auto-accept (CUR-05): off by default, and only at a threshold where the latest evaluation measured at least 99% precision over 50 or more decisions. Each sync accepts qualifying suggestions without conflicting evidence as one batch, marked **Auto-confirmed**, never used for learning, and undone in one step. A new model, or undoing a batch, switches it off. Ledger migration 0006.
- Local accounts and roles (PLT-06, CUR-06): set `[server] accounts = true` to require sign-in. Roles add up: viewers read, researchers also review the suggestions about their own profile (**My suggestions**), curators decide for everyone or only for one unit or person, and admins also manage accounts (**Admin → Accounts**, or `tessera users add | list | disable | enable | reset-password | roles`). Decisions record the signed-in person's name. Passwords are hashed with the standard library's scrypt; sessions are server-side, in `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Strict` cookies, with a CSRF token on every change and throttled sign-in. Every API route has an access policy, and a test fails for any route without one. Ledger migration 0005; ADR-0009.
- Possible duplicates: two records of one person (typically a roster row and a grant record with no shared identifier) are proposed as a pair, scored by `same_as/v1` on shared identifiers, a replaced ORCID iD, author positions both claim, shared grants, and names; never on a name alone. A new **Possible duplicates** page under Curate shows both records side by side with the evidence. `GET /api/v1/duplicates`.
- Reversible merges: merging moves a record's links, names, and identifiers to the record kept and journals every change, so **Undo merge** restores both exactly. Where both records decided the same link differently, the more recent decision holds and the merge says so. Merge from the duplicates page or with **Merge…** on a profile; merges appear in the decision log. `POST /api/v1/merges`, `POST /api/v1/merges/{id}/undo`; ledger migration 0004 (a `merged` status, `person.merged_into`, and `person_merge`).
- Dated unit memberships: roster `start` and `end` columns (a year, month, or day), several units at once, titles and dates on profiles, and only current units in the directory and its filters. People whose memberships have all ended become former members; importing an updated roster updates dates.
- ORCID iDs suggested from people's own papers: an iD printed at a person's author position on at least two of their likely papers is suggested on their profile, with how many papers carry it and what the public ORCID record says. Curators confirm it, mark it as not theirs, or add identifiers by hand; every decision is logged. An iD recorded for two people is flagged on both profiles. `POST /api/v1/people/{id}/identifiers`; ledger migration 0003.
- ORCID evidence in authorship scoring (`authorship/v1.1`): papers listed on a person's public ORCID record (+6.0), iDs a curator rejected (−8.0, like a conflict), and the same iD on the person's other papers (+2.0 or +3.0) or a clearly different one (−2.0, weak, since some people have two iDs). Deprecated iDs count as the iD that replaced them; an iD suggested for the person is never a conflict. On the pilot, the strong band grew from 1,652 to 1,666 suggestions, and the near misses on a short, common name left it.
- Papers listed on members' ORCID records are fetched from PubMed when the lake lacks them.
- ORCID connector: the public records of directory members who have an ORCID iD (works with their PubMed IDs and DOIs, and employment), read without credentials, refreshed every 30 days, and part of `tessera sync`. Deprecated, locked, and missing records are kept as data. New lake tables `orcid_record`, `orcid_work`, and `orcid_employment`; a `[sources.orcid]` setting.

## 0.1.0 (2026-11-07)

The first release. Tessera builds an organization's research graph from public data, with the evidence for every link, and gives curators fast tools to confirm it. It runs on a laptop or a single server.

**Highlights:** connectors for PubMed, iCite, NIH RePORTER, and ClinicalTrials.gov; a people directory from grant investigators, an optional roster, and discovered authors; explainable authorship suggestions; links between papers, grants, and studies; a fast, accessible web app in three palettes with light and dark versions; a keyboard-driven review queue; a JSON API; and user guides.

### Evaluation

- **Pilot check against ORCID iDs** at a mid-sized research institute (70 directory members, about 2,400 publications over five years; 588 matched papers labeled by the ORCID iD printed on them): the strong band was 98.3% precise at 93.7% recall (target 99%), and suggestions above the review threshold were 97.9% precise at 95.8% recall (target 90%). Seven of the nine strong-band disagreements appear to be people with a second ORCID iD rather than wrong matches. Details are in [spec/evidence-engine.md](spec/evidence-engine.md#pilot-check-against-orcid-ids-v010).
- **Synthetic organization with known answers:** the strong band is 100% precise with at least 99.8% recall, and candidate retrieval recall is 100%.
- **Speed on the pilot:** profile pages in 6 ms and search in 20 ms at the 95th percentile; in the review queue, the next suggestion's evidence appears in 22 ms and a decision is saved in 56 ms.

### Dependencies and licenses

Runtime dependencies: Alembic, DuckDB, FastAPI (with Starlette and Pydantic), HTTPX, PyArrow, SQLAlchemy, and Uvicorn, plus psycopg for the optional `postgres` extra. All are permissively licensed (MIT, BSD, Apache-2.0, PSF), except certifi (MPL-2.0, used unmodified) and psycopg (LGPL-3.0, optional and not bundled), both compatible with distributing Tessera under Apache-2.0. The web app ships no third-party code, fonts, or images.
